# Commit-message format for repositories where agents write and read most of the history

Final report of a research exercise on one question: what commit-message format will serve best when coding agents write most commits and are major readers of the history, with "follow the repository's existing style" ruled out as an answer. Written 2 October 2026 and revised three times the same day: after a first independent cross-check (CHECK-1); after a second cross-check (CHECK-2) together with a devil's-advocate round in which two advocates drafted the typed rival and four judges decided whether a typed header should be the default; and after a third cross-check of the report and its guidance distillation (CHECK-3). Every source cited was accessed that day by an evidence collector, a judge, an advocate, a checker or the synthesiser.

## The answer

Mandate one format across every repository the guidance covers, provisionally, with a typed Conventional Commits header available as a per-repository profile for a documented consumer: a bound type-reading tool, or a recorded need of the repository's readers to enumerate changes by kind for a named indexing, search, reporting or review workflow. The format, called Grounded Commits in the record, has an `area: outcome` subject with no change-type word; a prose body that opens with the problem or request and never narrates the diff, required except for a closed list of trivial mechanisms; obligations keyed on the claims the body makes (no behaviour change, defect repaired, performance effect); and a final Git trailer block carrying a nullable verification pair (`Verified:` / `Not-verified:`) with a fixed value grammar, `Refs:` as an absolute URL, `Fixes:` naming the introducing commit by hash, and `Breaking-Change:` when a public interface breaks. Under squash merging the pull-request title and description are the landing message, supplied explicitly through the forge API and read back. A hook and a landing check enforce shape; review and sampling check truth. The specification below is the round-three winner (P4) with the grafts the judges endorsed, its defects repaired, and the obligations the advocacy round contributed.

The reasoning: at commit time an agent holds four things it did not infer, the request, what it observed, what it changed and what it ran. The measured failures of generated messages are omitted context and unsupported rationale, not bad syntax, so the format asks for those four things and licenses "not run" and "reason not supplied" over a guess. The header carries an area and not a type on a cost judgement made under uncertainty, not on a measured balance: neither field has a measured reader benefit, and neither has a measured live error rate for a task-performing writer. What separates them is mechanism. An area is derivable from the changed paths by a stated procedure, so its classification burden has a mechanical floor; a type is an intent classification nothing derives. The type's deterministic consumers are release tools, which a profile binds where they exist. Agent commits are large and tangled (median 70 added lines in the one sampled agent population, tests folded into feature commits), and where they are squash-landed one type per landing loses information under any priority rule; how much of the adopting repositories' history squashes is a question for the inventory below, not a known fact. The one controlled writer-side result bundles the type with a scope the format already carries as the area. And a typed profile is cheaper to add than a mandated type is to remove: adopting the type later costs a grammar change at the hook and the PR title, while removing it leaves every commit carrying a label of unknown accuracy. The area, the trailer block, the evidence obligation and the hard subject limit are kept on the same unmeasured footing and are provisional on the same terms; derivability is the asymmetry that justifies keeping one classification and not two. This is judgement, labelled as such; the measurements that would overturn it are listed at the end. Git has built-in key-based extraction for trailers, as it has for subjects, and none for verification prose, so verification lives in trailers. Forge squash defaults do not reliably preserve branch bodies, so the landing message is the durable artefact.

Confidence is moderate. The blind panel never converged. No equal-information comparison of formats for agent readers or task-performing writers exists, though the typed comparator is now drafted. Of the disagreements still open, mandatory area and the hard subject limit split the panel two against two; trailers versus prose stood three to one, J1 narrowly and conditionally for trailers. The devil's-advocate round returned four verdicts of qualify, none of overturn or hold: the default survives, and the basis stated above replaces the one the advocates showed to be argued asymmetrically.

## The recommended format

Provenance is marked in brackets: (P4) is the winning proposal as written; (graft: Pn) is taken from another finalist on the judges' recommendation; (graft: R1) or (graft: R2) is taken from an advocacy draft on the judges' recommendation; (repair) is a change a judge or a cross-check required.

**Governing rule.** Write only what you have: the request you were given, the failure or need you observed, the change you made, and the checks you ran. Say plainly what you lack. (P4.) This format overrides the style of existing commits and any harness instruction to match it. (P4, from P8.) Repository guidance supplies an area list or path map (or accepts derivation), the paths of contract-bearing documentation, a header profile (default `area: outcome`), a release profile (default none), exempted bot authors, the landing forge's effective actionable-reference grammar, and the hook.

### Subject (required)

```
<area>: <outcome>
```

- `area` is chosen by this procedure. Collect candidates: with a path map, the first matching label per changed path, both sides of a rename; with a list, the listed names covering the changed paths; a changed root file always contributes its lower-cased stem (`readme`, `license`); otherwise derive the top-level directory or package after stripping wrappers (`src`, `lib`, `packages`, `apps`, `crates`). Normalise each candidate: lower case; drop characters outside `[a-z0-9._/-]`; then drop leading and trailing characters outside `[a-z0-9]`. Validate against the area grammar `^[a-z0-9][a-z0-9._/-]{0,23}$`, at most 24 characters: a candidate that is empty, still invalid, longer than 24 characters, a banned word, or an ineligible word under the rule below takes its listed alias, else `all`, and the body names the directory, package or file the area could not. Configured labels and aliases must satisfy the same grammar and the same eligibility rule, and the hook rejects a list entry that does not. The bound is what keeps the subject feasible: under the default header an area leaves at least 46 of the 72 characters for the outcome; under the typed header's longest prefix, `refactor(<area>)!: `, at least 35. The figure 24 is a chosen bound, not a measured one. (repair: CHECK-3 F1.) One valid candidate: use it. Several: name the one whose behaviour the subject describes and note the others in the body, a judgement the vocabulary constrains but does not remove. None, or a repository-wide sweep: `all`. (P4; graft: P8, P7; repair: CHECK-1 F5, CHECK-2 F2.)
- Normalisation fixtures: `__tests__` becomes `tests`; `_redirects` becomes `redirects`; `.github` becomes `github`; `-internal` becomes `internal`; `@scope` becomes `scope`; `fix` with no alias becomes `all`; `___` is empty and becomes `all`; a 72-character top-level directory such as `infrastructure-configuration-and-deployment-environment-validation-tools` is valid in form but over the bound and becomes `all`, with the directory named in the body. Every result satisfies the grammar or falls back, and every result leaves room for an outcome; nothing the procedure yields is rejected by the hook. (repair: CHECK-2 F2; CHECK-3 F1.)
- The area may not be `feat`, `fix`, `perf`, `chore`, `refactor`, `style` or `revert`. The words `ci`, `docs`, `test` and `build` are eligible only when they name a changed directory, a path-map label or a listed area; a root-file stem or a derived candidate that yields one of them without that backing is ineligible and takes the same route as a banned word: its listed alias, else `all`, with the file named in the body. Eligibility is checked in the validation step above, alongside the grammar, so nothing the procedure yields is rejected by the hook. Fixture: a commit confined to a root `build.gradle` derives the stem `build`, which names no changed directory, so the subject is `all: pin the Gradle wrapper to 8.14` with the file named in the body; a commit under a `build/` directory may use `build`. (P4, P8; repair: CHECK-4 F1.)
- `outcome` says what is now true or what the change does; lower case after the colon; no full stop, issue key, emoji, `!` or CI token.
- Hard limit 72 characters; aim for 60 to leave room for a forge's ` (#N)` suffix. (P4, P8.)

### Body (required unless the change is trivial)

Prose at 72 columns, no headings, in this order:

1. **Problem or trigger.** What was wrong, missing or requested; how it showed; where the request came from; the identifiers a searcher would type, inside sentences that survive a link dying. Distinguish request, observation and inference: "the requirement is", "the reproducer shows", "this is expected to". (P4; graft: P2 cue phrases, P8 identifiers.)
2. **Change and approach.** What now happens, as behaviour, and why this way. Keep symptoms and resulting behaviour even where the diff shows them; delete sentences that merely enumerate edits. (P4, from P8; repair: J3.)
3. Optional. **Considered and rejected:** beginning with those words; one alternative per sentence; only alternatives actually weighed. (P4.)
4. Optional. **Limits**, including a compatibility break: name the interface and the migration, or say the migration path is unknown. (P4, from P5.)

Every reason traces to a failure you observed, an issue or requirement you were pointed at, or an instruction in your brief; if the only reason you hold is that the brief asked, write that and cite it. A supplied diagnosis remains a hypothesis until observed. A writer without the task record writes "reason not available to the writer". Soft budget of about ten lines; identifiers and migration detail are never cut to meet it. (P4, P5, P7.)

**Claims carry obligations.** The kind of change is a claim the body makes, and each claim owes the evidence block something. (graft: R2, keyed on the claim the body makes rather than on a header token; repair: J4.)

- A body that claims no behaviour change says so in those words, "no behaviour change intended", and the evidence covers the existing suite on the final contents or declares the gap with that scope: `Not-verified: behaviour preservation; not run: <reason>` where the suite was not run, `Not-verified: behaviour preservation; result unavailable: <reason>` where it ran and the outcome cannot be recovered. A writer unsure whether behaviour changed states the intent and declares the gap; uncertainty is disclosed in the trailer, not resolved by claiming something else.
- A body that claims a defect is repaired names the symptom and how it showed, and the evidence covers the reproducer or declares the gap with the scope `reproduction`, in whichever form is true. `Fixes:` is added only with the substantiation below.
- A body that claims a performance effect names the property, and the evidence gives the before-and-after measurement or declares the gap with the scope `performance effect`, in whichever form is true. Figures are never supplied from memory or estimate.
- A body that claims a capability was added names where the request came from, which part 1 already requires.

Under the typed header profile the type is the first such claim and selects the obligation; under the default header the claim is read from the body. Each obligation accepts either declared `Not-verified:` form, chosen by what is known: `not run` when the check was not performed, `result unavailable` when it ran and the outcome cannot be recovered, as when CI ran the suite on the retained tree but its log expired before landing and no runner was available for another execution. Writing `not run` in that state would misstate the history, so the unavailable form is the accurate one. (repair: CHECK-3 F2.)

```
Not-verified: behaviour preservation; result unavailable: the CI
  result log expired before landing and no runner was available
```

**Trivial exception.** The body is omitted only when the repository's behaviour does not change and the subject names one of: typo, broken link, formatting, comment wording, rename with no callers changed, regenerated artefact. Line count never establishes triviality. A dependency bump is never trivial: one body line states the reason and the evidence pair is mandatory. (P4, list from P6.) Repair: the exception removes the body only; on non-documentation paths and contract-bearing documentation paths the evidence pair is still required, closing the loophole J4 identified. A trivial change on a code path implicitly claims no behaviour change, so its evidence pair covers the suite on the final contents or declares `Not-verified: behaviour preservation` in either form.

### Trailers (one final block, blank line before it, continuation lines indented)

| Key | Value | When |
|---|---|---|
| `Verified:` | `<tested contents>; <command or scenario>; <observed outcome>` | Repeatable |
| `Not-verified:` | `<scope>; not run: <reason>` or `<scope>; result unavailable: <reason>` | Repeatable |
| `Refs:` | Absolute URL of the issue, PR or discussion | Repeatable; never a closing keyword |
| `Fixes:` | 12 to 64 hex characters and the quoted subject of the commit that introduced the defect | With the substantiation below; never an issue number |
| `Breaking-Change:` | What breaks and what the consumer must do | Whenever a public interface changes incompatibly |
| `Changelog:` | one of `added fixed changed removed deprecated security performance other` | Release profile (b) only; exactly when user-visible in the released artefact |

The value grammar is grafted from P2; every judge named it graft-worthy. `final contents` means the tree of the commit that carries the message, and marks that the retained change was tested. Empty values, bare "passed" and `N/A` are invalid and rejected by the checks below. The hash range admits SHA-256 names. (repair: J1, J3, J4; CHECK-1 F6.)

**Delimiters.** A `Verified:` value is split at its first and its last semicolon: the tested-contents part and the outcome part contain no semicolon, and the command part between them may contain any, so `sh -c 'check_a; check_b'` needs no escaping. A `Not-verified:` value is split at the first `; not run:` or `; result unavailable:`; the scope contains no semicolon and the reason may. Git's `--parse` extracts the value as text and validates none of this; the three-part check is the format's own contract, applied by the hook. (repair: J1, J3.)

**Evidence rules.** At least one `Verified:` or `Not-verified:` is required on every commit that changes behaviour or touches code, tests, executable examples, configuration, schemas, dependency resolutions, generated runtime artefacts, build definitions, or a documented public contract, including one stated in documentation; when uncertain, include it. (P4; graft: P2; repair: J3, J4.) A result must cover the committed tree; if edits followed the check, re-run or write `Not-verified:` naming the difference. A CI or second-hand result is reported as such: `Verified: <sha>; CI run <url>; passed (reported)`. A writer without the session writes `Not-verified: <scope>; result unavailable: no session record available to the writer`, with the scope an obligation names where the body makes that claim and `changed behaviour` otherwise. (P4, P2, P7, P8; repair: CHECK-3 F2.)

**Evidence under rewrites.** Rebase, cherry-pick, squash and amend create new commits from existing message text. The writer who creates the new commit, the landing agent under squash, owns the evidence block of the result and rebinds it. A `Verified: final contents` line is kept only where the retained tree is the tree the check ran on (`git rev-parse <a>^{tree}` equal to `<b>^{tree}`, which a message-only amend leaves true) or where the check was re-run on the retained tree. The comparison decides, never the category of rewrite: a reorder, even on an unchanged base, may preserve the final branch tree while changing each replayed commit's tree, and Git's manual warns that "Reordering and editing commits usually creates untested intermediate steps" (https://git-scm.com/docs/git-rebase, Interactive mode); a replay onto a moved base ordinarily changes the tree even when the patch applied cleanly, which is exactly the case of a changed dependency resolution, so it needs a re-run or a rebinding unless the comparison shows the trees equal. Rebinding replaces `final contents` with the tested commit's identity, which may no longer be reachable after the rewrite, and either adds a `Not-verified:` scoped to the retained result or omits the obsolete claim. A line reading `Verified: final contents` therefore has one meaning under every rewrite: the tree of the commit that carries it was tested. (P2, P7; repair: CHECK-2 F3; CHECK-3 F5.)

```
Verified: 7c0d2e5a91b4 (the tested commit before rebase onto main);
  cargo test -p app-cli; 12 passed
Not-verified: final contents; not run: the branch was rebased onto
  main after the check and the suite was not re-run
```

`Fixes:` requires the defect shown present at the named commit and absent at its parent, by bisect or by running the reproducer at both, stated in the body; blame alone reports a line's last modification, not the defect's introduction. `git rev-parse --verify '<id>^{commit}'` proves the object exists, not ancestry or cause. Under squash landing name the landed commit. If copying the original subject would reproduce a forbidden token, omit the trailer and give the id in prose. (P4, P2; graft: P7.)

**Keys written by others.** Keys appended by a forge, harness or profile (`Co-Authored-By`, `Assisted-by`, a human's `Signed-off-by`, `Reviewed-on`, `Reviewed-by`, `Change-Id`, session links) follow their consumer's documented meaning, cardinality and rewrite rule, not a blanket preservation rule: a squash keeps exactly one `Change-Id`, that of the review being landed, and the superseded reviews are abandoned in Gerrit; duplicate `Co-Authored-By` lines are removed; harness attribution is kept as emitted, corrected when known false, never relied on for provenance. A `(cherry picked from commit …)` line may follow the block. Any further key needs a named consumer and a documented contract. An agent never adds `Signed-off-by` or infers approval. (P4, P3, P2; graft: P2 and P7 reconciliation; repair: J1, J3; CHECK-1 F7.)

### Native Git messages

Generated merge and revert subjects are kept; an integration merge with no new resolution may omit a body. A revert body states the original hash and subject, why, and whether the reversal is complete or partial; a partial reversal must not pair a `Revert` header with the generated "This reverts commit" sentence, which the Angular revert pattern and semantic-release's filter read as complete. `fixup!`, `squash!` and `amend!` messages are exempt until incorporated. (P4, P2, P7; repair: J2.)

### Header profile and release profile (each chosen once, recorded in guidance)

Two decisions, recorded separately. The **header profile** is the subject grammar: the default above, or the typed header. The **release profile** is what, if anything, automates versions and notes: default none. A typed header does not activate release automation; a release tool that reads types is one of two reasons to adopt the typed header. (repair: J1, J3.)

**Typed header profile.** Chosen where a type-reading tool is bound (release profile (c)), or where guidance records that the repository's readers need to enumerate changes by kind from subjects, for a named indexing, search, reporting or review workflow. Recorded in guidance either way, with the consuming workflow named. (repair: J1, J2, J3, J4.) Under it:

- Subject `type(area)!?: outcome`. The area is chosen by the procedure above and the parentheses are always present, `fix(all): …` included, so one header shape and one enumeration regex serve every commit. (graft: R1.) Hook grammar: `^(feat|fix|perf|refactor|docs|test|build|ci)\([a-z0-9][a-z0-9._/-]{0,23}\)!?: \S`; the area bound leaves at least 35 characters for the outcome under the longest prefix. The area ban on type words is lifted; the type occupies its own position.
- Vocabulary: Angular's eight, `feat`, `fix`, `perf`, `refactor`, `docs`, `test`, `build`, `ci`; no `chore`, `style` or invented type. Angular removed `chore` as "overused by everyone on the team" (https://github.com/angular/angular/commit/dff6ee32725197bdb81f3f63c5bd9805f2ed22bb). The vocabulary has no residual: a behaviour change that is neither a defect repair nor a performance change, a requested removal or a default change included, is `feat` with the request's source named, which is why the drift monitor below watches the `feat` share. (graft: R1, R2; the gap is J4's finding.)
- Type procedure, purpose over object, the line Zeng et al. draw (https://0x404.github.io/assets/pdf/conventional_commit.pdf, §V-B): (1) if the behaviour of the running software changes: a defect you observed or were pointed at no longer occurs, `fix`; only a measured property improves, `perf`; otherwise `feat`. A change to a documented public contract is classified by this step as if it were the behaviour it documents. (2) If code changes and behaviour is intended unchanged: `refactor`, with the no-behaviour-change obligation; an unrun suite is declared in `Not-verified:`, not resolved by choosing another type. (3) If running behaviour does not change and every changed path is of one kind: `docs`, `test`, `build` (build definitions, manifests, lockfiles) or `ci`; a dependency bump that delivers a known fix or feature takes that type, otherwise `build`, and is never trivial. (4) A commit that mixes types is split before landing where you can; where you cannot, the retained type is the highest in the profile's priority order and the body names the rest. The order here is the report's own, `feat`, `fix`, `perf`, `refactor`, `build`, `ci`, `test`, `docs`, ranked by consequence for the shipped artefact so that the retained type is the one whose omission would under-bump a release tool; it is not Open edX's, whose OEP-51 reads "revert, feat, fix, perf, docs, test, build, refactor, style, chore, temp" (https://open-edx-proposals.readthedocs.io/en/latest/best-practices/oep-0051-bp-conventional-commits.html, fetched by J2 and J4). Any priority order is lossy: a repair landed inside a feature PR is retained as `feat`, so the enumeration handle under-recalls, which the fix-enumeration trial below measures. (graft: R2 procedure; repair: J2, J4.)
- Obligations: the type is the first claim the message makes and selects the obligation in the Body section. The hook checks that the evidence pair is present on every `fix`, `perf` and `refactor` whatever the paths; review checks what no hook can, that the symptom, the request's source or the intent are named and true. (graft: R2; repair: J2, J4.)
- Breaking marker: `!` before the colon, paired with the `Breaking-Change:` trailer; one without the other is rejected, except where the profile binds a parser that admits no `!`. semantic-release's default Angular preset and Lerna's default preset are such parsers: there the profile replaces both the header grammar and the pairing check, forbids `!`, and emits a spaced `BREAKING CHANGE:` paragraph last in the body, outside the trailer block. Accepting `!` in a header regex is not enough on its own: the bound parser must also produce a recognised breaking note, which the conventional-commits parser synthesises from its breaking-header pattern, and the analyser's major rule depends on a note being present, so a header-only configuration change is validated by fixture against the installed analyser and note renderer, never assumed (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/conventional-changelog/conventional-changelog/master/packages/conventional-commits-parser/src/CommitParser.ts; https://raw.githubusercontent.com/semantic-release/commit-analyzer/master/lib/analyze-commit.js, fetched by J3). (P4; graft: R1; repair: J1, J3, J4.)
- Under squash the type goes on the PR title, which becomes the retained subject; under merge or rebase, on each commit. (P4.)
- Drift monitor, a sampling signal: `git log --format=%s | sed 's/[(:!].*//' | sort | uniq -c` per period; a rising `feat` share or a vanishing `refactor` share is the cue to sample bodies against types. (graft: R2.)

**Release profile.** Default none. Where automation is wanted: (a) a tracked change file; (b) the `Changelog:` trailer where the tool reads trailers; (c) the typed header where bound to a type-reading tool, which presupposes the typed header profile. Version selection, changelog inclusion and package targeting are separate decisions: one authoritative bump input per release unit. (P4 menu; graft: P2 and P7, repairing J3's fatal finding that P4 demanded a "major" fixture from a changelog-only profile.) A profile names consumer and version, exact grammar, value meanings, and a validation command run on the landed message. A version-selecting profile passes fixtures against the installed analyser and note renderer: a `!` header and the spaced and hyphenated breaking notes under the installed preset; a no-release commit; a complete and a partial revert; a scope that is a focal area rather than a package name, and a change touching several packages, because Nx in independent mode with `useCommitScope: true` restricts bump magnitude to unscoped commits or a scope matching the project name, and package targeting is a contract of its own. Tool support is not activation: Nx reads Conventional Commits only with `release.version.conventionalCommits` enabled, Lerna only under `lerna version --conventional-commits`, whose default preset is Angular and admits no `!` until `--changelog-preset conventionalcommits` is selected (https://nx.dev/docs/guides/nx-release/automatically-version-with-conventional-commits; https://raw.githubusercontent.com/lerna/lerna/main/libs/commands/version/README.md). (graft: P2; repair: J1, J3, J4.) Profiles are version-controlled with their activation boundary recorded. (graft: P7.)

### Rewrites and migration

Rebase and cherry-pick reuse message text in new objects, so the evidence block is rebound as the evidence rules require and old signatures do not cover the new objects; `cherry-pick -x` adds a reference, not ancestry. Git notes travel only with their refs. PR text, reviews and forge verification records are forge state a clone does not carry; `Refs:` URLs survive as text, bare `#N` only if numbering is imported. Essential reasoning therefore stays in the body. (P4, P2, P7; https://git-scm.com/docs/git-cherry-pick; https://git-scm.com/docs/git-notes; https://git-scm.com/docs/git-clone; repair: CHECK-1 F7, CHECK-2 F3.)

### Forbidden

- A change-type prefix outside the typed header profile; issue keys or emoji in the subject.
- Narrating the diff; file lists as the body; a motive you did not receive; "tests pass" without naming the tests; a check you neither ran nor can cite.
- CI control tokens anywhere, even quoted (`[skip ci]` and variants, `skip-checks:`, `***NO_CI***`, any token the repository's CI documents); if a user wants one, the user commits it.
- Actionable issue references: any keyword the landing forge treats as closing or reopening, in that forge's effective list, followed by a reference the forge resolves. The lists differ by forge and are configurable, so guidance records the deployed forge's effective pattern and the hook carries it; the fixtures below are the minimum. GitHub's documented defaults are `close`, `closes`, `closed`, `fix`, `fixes`, `fixed`, `resolve`, `resolves`, `resolved`, with or without a colon, before `#N` or `OWNER/REPO#N` (https://docs.github.com/en/issues/tracking-your-work-with-issues/using-issues/linking-a-pull-request-to-an-issue). GitLab's defaults add the `-ing` forms and `implement`, `implements`, `implemented`, `implementing`, accept the full issue URL and, after a keyword, a Jira-style key, act when the commit reaches the default branch, and are changeable by a self-managed administrator (https://docs.gitlab.com/user/project/issues/managing_issues/#default-closing-pattern). Forgejo's defaults are GitHub's nine plus `reopen`, `reopens`, `reopened`, administrator-customisable (https://forgejo.org/docs/latest/user/collaboration/linked-references/). (repair: CHECK-1 for GitHub's list; CHECK-2 F1 for the cross-forge grammar; the GitLab and Forgejo pages were fetched by the checker and again by the synthesiser.)
- Instructions to future readers or agents; history records decisions, not standing orders.
- Session-local references: plan steps, scratch paths, tracking codes, model names in prose.

### Mechanical checks (a `commit-msg` hook locally; the same checks at landing)

Shape: subject within 72, matching `^([a-z0-9][a-z0-9._/-]*): \S`; the area satisfies the area grammar, 24 characters at most, whatever its source, and is listed, mapped, derived or `all`; native merge, revert, `fixup!`, `squash!` and `amend!` subjects exempt; under the typed header profile the profile's header grammar replaces this and the type-ban regex `^(feat|fix|perf|chore|refactor|style|revert)[(!:]`. A non-trivial commit has a prose paragraph before the trailers, and the block parses with `git interpret-trailers --parse`. Values: `Verified:` has three non-empty parts by the first-and-last-semicolon split and is not a filler word (`passed`, `ok`, `n/a`, `none`, `tests pass`, case-insensitive); `Not-verified:` has a scope then `; not run:` or `; result unavailable:` and a reason; `Refs:` matches `^https?://`; `Fixes:` matches `^[0-9a-f]{12,64} \(".+"\)$` and resolves. Evidence pair present when any changed path is outside documentation or inside the contract-bearing documentation paths guidance names, and, under the typed header profile, on every `fix`, `perf` and `refactor`. No CI token, case-insensitive; no actionable reference under the forge's effective pattern. Listed bot authors are exempt from body and evidence checks at commit time. Only the landing agent or reviewer can judge whether a documentation-only change outside the named paths alters a contract, whether a trivial mechanism was trivial, whether a claimed symptom or intent is true, or whether a check ran. A local hook is bypassed by `--no-verify` and reaches only commits made where it is installed; the landing check covers the rest. (P4; repair: J1, J3; CHECK-1 F6; CHECK-2 F1, F2.)

Fixtures the hook passes, as string checks against the documented grammars; no forge parser, release tool or agent was exercised (repair: CHECK-2 F1, F2; J1):

| `Fixes: 9f3c1a2b7d4e ("scheduler: release leases eagerly on shutdown")` | accepted: a hash is not an issue reference on any of the three |

## Why

Measurement, mechanism and opinion are separated below. Nothing in the collected evidence is an equal-information comparison of formats for agent readers or task-performing writers; post-hoc generation experiments exist and are reported where they bear.

### Agent writers

The format asks for what a task-performing agent has and a post-hoc generator lacks. Mechanism: Aider's auto-commit passes the chat history to the message call; VS Code's generator receives diffs and recent subjects, not the task conversation; opencode's GitHub handler asks for a summary under 40 characters (https://github.com/Aider-AI/aider/blob/5dc9490bb35f9729ef2c95d00a19ccd30c26339c/aider/coders/base_coder.py, https://github.com/microsoft/vscode/blob/261d41b4cc914eced6408665704d7742c473a5f6/extensions/copilot/src/extension/prompt/vscode-node/gitCommitMessageServiceImpl.ts; https://raw.githubusercontent.com/anomalyco/opencode/aec0b9a6d8898f68f923aaf08b7306d931fd9d76/packages/opencode/src/cli/cmd/github.handler.ts).

A problem-first body targets the measured failure. Measurement: of 108 ChatGPT-generated messages, 38 lacked context and 6 hallucinated rationale, overlapping categories from post-hoc generation, not a task-agent error rate (https://arxiv.org/html/2401.17622v2, Table 6). A required explanation field raised what-plus-why content from 17.2% to 76% in a student study that bundled labels, obligation and interface (https://arxiv.org/html/2304.13887). About 44% of 1,597 human messages lacked what or why (https://arxiv.org/html/2202.02974).

The nullable evidence pair rests on precedent and a transferred argument. Precedent: the kernel requires assistant-generated fixes to say so explicitly when the fix could not be built, tested or reproduced (https://docs.kernel.org/process/coding-assistants.html); Phabricator's required Test Plan records what was actually done (https://secure.phabricator.com/book/phabricator/article/differential_test_plans/). Both show prose can disclose a missing check; the trailer adds a standard key, compulsory presence in a defined scope, and a grammar a hook can reject. Theory: when abstention earns nothing, guessing is rational (https://arxiv.org/html/2509.04664v1, §4.2). Caution: required template questions can be skipped with `N/A`; that study measures conformance, not a filler rate (https://www.researchgate.net/publication/377860718_An_Empirical_Analysis_of_Issue_Templates_Usage_in_Large-Scale_Projects_on_GitHub). Self-reports also inherit a known unfaithfulness: of 426 explanations supporting biased answers, one mentioned the bias (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.04388v2; https://arxiv.org/html/2505.05410v1), which is why `Verified:` is a claim, not an attestation. The claim-keyed obligations extend the same design to the kind of change: a claim with a named honest exit (graft: R2).

The type is left out of the default header on the cost judgement stated in the answer; the measurements bear on that judgement less than the first revision of this report implied, in both directions. Against the type stand three different quantities, none of them a live error rate for a writer that holds the intent: the best classifier on Zeng et al.'s 2,000 adjudicated single-type commits, given the message with its type removed plus the diff, reached macro-F1 76.41% with `chore` at 53.97% (https://0x404.github.io/assets/pdf/conventional_commit.pdf, §VI); three LLMs scored 0.608–0.638 accuracy on CommitSuite's 1,160 checked examples (https://arxiv.org/html/2605.02256v1, §3.4, Table 8); CoCoRerank's reranked generated type agreed with normalised references 48.52% of the time (https://arxiv.org/html/2609.30953v1, Table 2). For the type, nothing isolates it. CoCoRerank's ablation prompted three small code models for type, scope and subject together against the subject alone and raised subject overlap with the developer's message for all three (Qwen2.5-Coder BLEU 20.86 to 23.37, ROUGE-L 14.76 to 15.64; mean relative ROUGE-L gain 10.08%; Table 6); the authors' sentence is "jointly generating type and scope provides useful structural guidance for producing higher-quality subjects", and their case study credits the scope with anchoring the subject while saying of the type that "although refactor is not the most accurate type, it reflects that the change mainly reorders existing statements rather than adding new behavior, encouraging the subject to describe the altered execution order" (fetched by J2, J4 and the synthesiser). J4 read the type-steering and the scope-anchoring sentences as both fair readings of one paragraph; J3 held R2's type-steering reading too strong; the authors' interpretation is qualitative, not a controlled estimate of the type's own effect (repair: CHECK-3 F6). The ablation supports a structured target that includes a scope, which this format carries as the area; it does not lean towards the type over the area, and the first revision of this report was wrong to say it did (repair: J2, J4). Nothing measures area selection accuracy either. For writers the record is a wash between the two headers; the decision rests on the mechanisms stated in the answer, and the three-arm trial below is what would move it.

The precedence clause and hook exist because both inspected harnesses instruct imitation of recent messages, which propagates whatever prefixes a history has. Measurement: 513 of 790 sampled Claude-attributed commits (65%) carried a Conventional prefix; the cause is unresolved, and the paper allows imitation of project style or explicit instruction (https://arxiv.org/html/2601.18341v2 §10). Neither Claude Code v2.1.49, v2.1.287 nor opencode v1.18.34 mentions Conventional Commits; both ask for concise prose in repository style (https://registry.npmjs.org/@anthropic-ai/claude-code/-/claude-code-2.1.49.tgz; https://raw.githubusercontent.com/anomalyco/opencode/aec0b9a6d8898f68f923aaf08b7306d931fd9d76/packages/opencode/src/tool/shell/shell.txt). The rejection-and-retry cost of enforcing the ban against that prior is unmeasured and is counted on the verdict's side of the ledger below. Airflow's merged hook rejecting type prefixes while allowing area prefixes is the precedent (https://github.com/apache/airflow/pull/68633).

### Agent readers

Retrieval is lexical before it is semantic. Mechanism: Code Researcher selects commits by regex over messages and patches; RepoMem ranks messages with BM25 and builds its memory from explicit commit–issue links, illustrated in its appendix by messages containing "Fixes #123", with the warning that absent links lower retrieval recall (https://arxiv.org/html/2506.11060v1; https://arxiv.org/html/2510.01003v2, §3.1 and Appendix A.1). This format forbids that syntax where the forge would act on it and carries the link as a `Refs:` URL; whether that construction's link extractor accepts an absolute URL is untested, a gap J3 named in round three and the second check restored (repair: CHECK-2 F5). Lexical matching of message text and extraction of commit–issue relationships are different paths; the reader trial below covers the second. A stable line-start token, searchable identifiers and fixed trailer keys are what the first path matches. Opinion, held by two judges and denied by two: a token present on every commit is a handle; one present "when it helps" is a mixture. No retrieval measurement exists for either position.

One reader query goes unserved under this format (repair: J2, J4). "Which commits were defect repairs" or "which added a capability" has no handle: the reader scans subjects (`--format=%s`, about 60 bytes a commit, so 10,000 commits cost about 600 kB) for keywords of unknown recall, or reads bodies (about 1,200 bytes each, about 12 MB for the same history). A typed header answers it with a line-start match over the same subject scan, at a precision and recall bounded by the priority rule under squash. The archaeology results below both start from a known fix commit, which is this query's output, not its input. The cost is accepted for the default; a repository for which that query dominates records the need and takes the typed header profile.

Message content helps machine archaeology. Measurement: removing the fix message dropped SZZ-Agent's F1 from 0.81 to 0.73 on 100 Linux fixes with `Fixes:` lines redacted (https://arxiv.org/html/2603.29378v1, Table 4); LLMBisect found 15 bug-introducing commits only through its message component (https://www.cs.ucr.edu/~zhiyunq/pub/ndss26_llmbisect.pdf). Both show information helping, not a grammar.

Selective extraction bounds reading cost. Mechanism: Git exposes the subject through `%s` and recognised trailer fields through `%(trailers:key=Verified,valueonly,unfold)` without emitting the body; prose verification has no such handle; `--grep` matches body lines, so subject filtering uses `--format=%s` (https://git-scm.com/docs/pretty-formats; https://git-scm.com/docs/git-log). Arithmetic, not measurement: the behavioural examples above are about 1,200 bytes each, so reading every body of 10,000 commits costs millions of tokens; no tokeniser was run.

Directives are forbidden because history is an injection channel. Fact: a planted commit message steered Cursor into an unintended Git MCP operation carrying a shell payload (https://github.com/cyanheads/git-mcp-server/security/advisories/GHSA-3q26-f695-pp76); GitHub's MCP server lists commits among untrusted content (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/github/github-mcp-server/main/README.md); Lore's reader instructions say of `Directive`, "Follow it. Treat as a standing order." (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Ian-stetsenko/lore-protocol/main/skills/lore-agent-instructions.md). A "Considered and rejected" paragraph is a dated decision; the kernel reversed its own attribution rule within seven months (https://github.com/torvalds/linux/commit/816d9992d9ed434ec52cfbd63080d518e535a41b). Git's manual says blame reports the last modification of surviving lines, not the introduction, hence the parent-and-commit rule for `Fixes:` (https://git-scm.com/docs/git-blame).

### Human readers and reviewers

Reviewers want motivation, and rationale is what people cannot answer later. Measurement: description was the most raised reviewability aspect and nine of ten interviewees wanted motivation as well as what changed (https://anandsaw.github.io/publications/fse2018.pdf); unfamiliar reviewers read the description first (https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/ICSE202013-codereview.pdf); of 371 hard-question reports from 179 developers, rationale was the largest category (42); "Why wasn't it done this other way?" (15) edged "Why was it done this way?" (14) (https://ecs.wgtn.ac.nz/foswiki/pub/Events/PLATEAU/2010Program/plateau10-latoza.pdf, Figure 1, §4.1.4). Of observed searches for implementation rationale, 44% were deferred or abandoned (https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2007/05/icse07_ko.pdf). Of 217 survey respondents, 66% named uninformative messages as a problem, a self-report (https://dig.cs.illinois.edu/papers/codoban-icsme15.pdf); one Microsoft survey rated rationale easy to obtain where descriptions were informative (https://people.csail.mit.edu/hunkim/papers/tao-fse2012.pdf). Codoban's interviewees narrow history and skim messages before investigating intent, which is testimony about a scan-then-investigate workflow and not about the feature they filter on; Open edX adopted typed headers for its readers before its tools (OEP-51), which is a project's stated rationale. That a kind-announcing token is what such a reader would filter on is the advocates' proposed application of those two facts, and J4 noted that the Codoban testimony does not establish it; comprehension is unmeasured under either header. (repair: CHECK-4 F3.)

Subject length rests on display contracts, not comprehension. Fact: GitHub's title-based squash puts the "pull request title and number on the first line" (https://github.blog/changelog/2022-08-23-new-options-for-controlling-the-default-commit-message-when-merging-a-pull-request/, re-fetched by two judges); GitLens 19.3.0's default blame field is 50 characters including its separator (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/gitkraken/vscode-gitlens/v19.3.0/package.json). Arithmetic: `parser: ` costs 8 of those columns, `fix(parser): ` 13. No comprehension measurement exists for any limit.

### Machine consumers

Trailers follow Git's rules. Fact: a block is recognised only as the final paragraph after a blank line; keys are alphanumerics and hyphens; the spaced `BREAKING CHANGE:` is not a trailer; a blank line before an appended paragraph drops every earlier key; `--parse` extracts fields and validates no values (https://git-scm.com/docs/git-interpret-trailers; fixtures run on Git 2.55.0 during evidence collection and in CHECK-1 and CHECK-2).

Parsers differ, and support is not activation. Fact, from source the judges fetched: the Angular preset's header admits no `!` and its note keywords are `['BREAKING CHANGE']`; the conventionalcommits preset admits `!` and lists `BREAKING-CHANGE`, matched case-insensitively, so `Breaking-Change:` is read there and ignored under Angular (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/conventional-changelog/conventional-changelog/master/packages/conventional-changelog-angular/src/parser.js; https://raw.githubusercontent.com/conventional-changelog/conventional-changelog/master/packages/conventional-changelog-conventionalcommits/src/parser.js). A header regex that accepts `!` is not a breaking contract on its own: the conventional-commits parser synthesises the breaking note from its breaking-header pattern, the analyser's major rule looks for a note, and the note renderer must then preserve it (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/conventional-changelog/conventional-changelog/master/packages/conventional-commits-parser/src/CommitParser.ts; https://raw.githubusercontent.com/semantic-release/commit-analyzer/master/lib/analyze-commit.js, fetched by J3). semantic-release filters recognised revert pairs (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/semantic-release/commit-analyzer/master/index.js); release-please reads `Release-As` and a PR-body override and "highly" recommends squash (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/googleapis/release-please/main/README.md); GitLab's changelog reads a `Changelog:` trailer with the version as explicit input (https://docs.gitlab.com/user/project/changelogs/); Changesets stores per-package bump levels and prose in files, which a single commit type does not encode (https://changesets.dev/guide/why). Nx reads types only when `release.version.conventionalCommits` is enabled and, in independent mode with `useCommitScope: true`, restricts bump magnitude to unscoped commits or a scope matching the project name, so a focal area is not a package target; Lerna reads types only under `--conventional-commits`, with Angular as its default preset (https://nx.dev/docs/guides/nx-release/automatically-version-with-conventional-commits; https://raw.githubusercontent.com/lerna/lerna/main/libs/commands/version/README.md). Gerrit matches a review on `Change-Id`, repository and branch, and after a squash wants exactly one `Change-Id` kept (https://gerrit-review.googlesource.com/Documentation/user-changeid.html).

Squash defaults do not reliably preserve branch bodies. Fact, from pinned source and documentation: Forgejo v16.0.5's default web squash copies the PR description, not commit bodies, and rebuilds co-authors from Git author fields; its bare API merge uses the title plus only the caller's `MergeMessageField`; GitLab's default template is `%{title}`; GitHub keeps the sole commit's message for a one-commit PR but lists commits for more, unless `PR_BODY` is configured (https://code.forgejo.org/forgejo/forgejo/raw/commit/8d24b7d52df327118cd776cf461d34e52e0ec1ce/services/pull/pull.go; https://code.forgejo.org/forgejo/forgejo/raw/commit/8d24b7d52df327118cd776cf461d34e52e0ec1ce/routers/api/v1/repo/pull.go; https://docs.gitlab.com/user/project/merge_requests/commit_templates/; https://docs.github.com/en/repositories/configuring-branches-and-merges-in-your-repository/configuring-pull-request-merges/configuring-commit-squashing-for-pull-requests). No route guarantees arbitrary trailer survival.

CI tokens act on substrings. Fact: Gitea v1.22.0 and Forgejo v7.0.0 match `SKIP_WORKFLOW_STRINGS` case-sensitively anywhere in the resolved message; GitHub reads any pushed commit message, or the PR HEAD, and honours a `skip-checks:` trailer; a suppressed workflow leaves required checks Pending rather than passing (https://github.com/go-gitea/gitea/blob/v1.22.0/services/actions/notifier_helper.go; https://docs.github.com/en/actions/how-tos/manage-workflow-runs/skip-workflow-runs; https://docs.github.com/en/pull-requests/how-tos/merge-and-close-pull-requests/troubleshooting-required-status-checks). Quoting does not neutralise a substring match; no report in the collected evidence shows an agent writing a skip token to clear remote CI.

Actionable references are forge contracts with different grammars. Fact: GitHub closes on nine keywords before `#N` or `OWNER/REPO#N`; GitLab's default pattern adds the `-ing` forms and the `implement` family, accepts a full issue URL or a Jira-style key, fires when the commit reaches the default branch, and is administrator-configurable; Forgejo adds reopening keywords and lets the administrator customise the list (sources in the Forbidden section). A fixed list is therefore a labelled example, and the deployed forge's effective pattern is the contract (repair: CHECK-2 F1).

Attribution is a claim: VS Code shipped a regression crediting non-Copilot completions to Copilot (https://github.com/microsoft/vscode/issues/314311); the kernel replaced model names with generic `LLM` as "little or no useful information" (https://github.com/torvalds/linux/commit/816d9992d9ed434ec52cfbd63080d518e535a41b); a signature authenticates the object, not its claims (https://git-scm.com/docs/gitformat-signature).

## Conventional Commits

Verdict: not the default, provisionally; permitted as the typed header profile where a type-reading tool is bound or where guidance records a reader need to enumerate changes by kind, with the parser's own breaking marker, claim-keyed obligations and fixture validation. The basis is the cost judgement in the answer. The first revision of this report stated it as "the one field Conventional Commits makes mandatory, the type, is the one field with measured classification difficulty and no measured reader benefit", which was wrong in two ways: the description is mandatory too (https://www.conventionalcommits.org/en/v1.0.0/, rules 1 and 5), and the standard did not distinguish the type from the area, which has no measured reader benefit either and an unmeasured, not absent, classification difficulty. Both advocates pressed this, as J1 and J3 had across three rounds of the main exercise; the panel accepted it and so does this revision.

The round's shape: because every original proposal rejected the typed header, two advocates, one per model family, independently drafted Rich Conventional Commits, the equal-information typed rival, on this report's body, trailers, checks and landing rules (R1, R2); four judges, two per family, decided head-to-head whether a typed header should be the default. All four returned qualify: the default stands, its stated basis and the profile's conditions change. The arguments, by name.

**For keeping the default, what the judges found decisive.** *The derivability argument*: an area has a mechanical floor, a path map or a top-level directory, and a type has none; on a tie in evidence the default should carry the fewer per-commit judgements review must police (J2, J4). *The tangled-landing argument*: the one sampled agent population has a median of 70 added lines a commit with tests folded into feature commits (https://arxiv.org/html/2601.18341v2, Table 6), and, where it lands by squash, one type per landing loses information under any priority order; the squash share is unmeasured both in that sample, whose extraction records merge type without reporting it (§4.2.5), and in the repositories this format targets; the one agreement figure for the taxonomy was measured on single-type commits (J2, J4; repair: CHECK-3 F7). *The reversibility argument*: a typed profile is cheaper to add than a mandated type is to remove (J4). *The separate-contracts argument*: one header grammar does not create one release contract, since parser presets, package targeting and release policy still differ by consumer (J1, J3). *The parser-fragmentation point* is retained as an integration obligation the typed profile pays by binding and fixtures, not as evidence of an intrinsic penalty (J3).

**For the typed default, the advocates' strongest points and what this revision does with each.** *The asymmetric-standard argument* (R1, R2; J1 and J3 in rounds one to three): correct, accepted, and the basis restated above. *The kind-enumeration argument* (R2's best mechanism point): the archaeology results this report cites start from a known fix commit; when the task does not already supply the relevant fixes (an incident record or a user-named commit does), the agent must first find them, and a line-start token is one way the set is found; the first revision had no answer. J1 and J3 rejected R2's stronger claim that nobody hands an agent that commit, as a universal precondition the cited research does not establish, while both supported allowing a non-release type consumer; the narrower upstream task stands. (repair: CHECK-4 F4.) Accepted as a real unserved query, priced in the reader section, and made a trigger for the typed profile; its limit is recall under the priority rule, which the fix-enumeration trial measures, and R2's own enumeration command missed its unscoped form (J1, J3, J4). *The type-as-claim argument* (R2): a type with a per-type obligation and an honest exit is a better design than a bare label; grafted, keyed on the claim the body makes so the default format gains it without the header (J4). *The familiar-prior argument* (R1, R2): 65% of one agent's sampled commits carry a type unasked by the harness, and fighting that prior has a cost; accepted as an unmeasured cost on the verdict's side, with the counter that aligning with the prior is equally unmeasured and prevalence is not quality (J2, J4). *The annotator-agreement argument* (R2): a defined taxonomy was applied with kappa 0.9; accepted as evidence the taxonomy is applicable, limited by the conditions below (J1, J3, J4). *The one-grammar argument* (R2): one grammar everywhere beats two if most repositories bind a type-reading tool; nobody has the inventory, so the inventory decides, under a rule stated before it is read (J1, J2).

The nine criticisms in the record, re-assessed for whether they discriminate between this format and the rich typed rival:

1. **Type choice is confused.** Measured, with limits. Zeng et al. screened 194 GitHub issues and 100 Stack Overflow questions down to 52 classification problem reports, self-selected, of which 30 concerned type confusion; 22 of the 30 concerned scenarios the specification leaves undefined (a submodule update, an initial commit) and 8 the definitions (https://0x404.github.io/assets/pdf/conventional_commit.pdf, §V-A). The paper's remedy is a definition list with purpose-over-object precedence, under which two annotators, the first and third authors, who wrote the definitions, disagreed on 79 of 2,000 commits, Cohen's kappa 0.9, on single-type commits stratified by the developer's own label with `chore` and `style` available as residuals (§VI; fetched by J2 and J4). The best classifier on that benchmark reached macro-F1 76.41% with `chore` at 53.97% (§VI); three LLMs scored 0.608–0.638 on CommitSuite (https://arxiv.org/html/2605.02256v1); generated type agreement reached 48.52% after reranking (https://arxiv.org/html/2609.30953v1). These are four quantities (problem-report counts, annotator agreement, classifier label recovery, generated agreement) and none is a live error rate for a task-performing agent. The transfer limit cuts both ways: the agreement figure does not reach an eight-type vocabulary with no residual applied by agents to tangled commits with a median of 70 added lines, squash-landed where repositories squash, and the classifier figures do not charge a writer who holds the intent with a post-hoc recovery rate. Angular removed `chore` as "overused by everyone on the team" (https://github.com/angular/angular/commit/dff6ee32725197bdb81f3f63c5bd9805f2ed22bb), a project response without a before-and-after measure. The strongest criticism, and still a cost judgement.
2. **The body is optional, so compliance certifies nothing a reader needs.** True of the core specification, "A longer commit body MAY be provided", where only `feat` and `fix` are defined (https://www.conventionalcommits.org/en/v1.0.0/). Irrelevant to a rich variant, which is the rival under consideration: Angular mandates a body except for `docs` (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/angular/angular/main/contributing-docs/commit-message-guidelines.md), Open edX requires a self-sufficient account, R1 removes the exemption and R2 narrows it. Not counted in the verdict.
3. **The prefix taxes the subject.** Arithmetic plus opinion (https://richvdh.org/conventional-commits-considered-harmful.html; https://sumnerevans.com/posts/software-engineering/stop-using-conventional-commits/): `fix(scheduler): ` costs 16 columns where `scheduler: ` costs 11. Reading cost and displaced identifiers are unmeasured, and this format accepts the area's columns for an equally unmeasured benefit. An unresolved trade-off, not a discriminator.
4. **Conventional messages are less informative.** Observational: in 50,673 security commits, conventional messages reached the "good" tier at 7.89% against 22.85% but "very poor" at 4.70% against 8.70%, confounded by ecosystem, era and author (https://arxiv.org/html/2604.20461v1, Table 6). The populations were not assigned identical bodies with different headers, so it cannot identify the effect of adding a type to the same grounded message. Retained as a warning that syntax compliance does not buy informativeness; not counted.
5. **Reverts break automated versioning.** Too broad: semantic-release filters recognised revert pairs (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/semantic-release/commit-analyzer/master/index.js), though not partial or manual reversals, a lifecycle problem change files and trailers share. Not counted.
6. **Type-triggered pipelines are a security hazard.** Hypothetical: a vendor recipe skips deployment for `docs`-type messages (https://www.deployhq.com/blog/conventional-commits-a-standardized-approach-to-commit-messages), but no bypass incident was found. The hazard belongs to any pipeline acting on self-declared text, which is why CI tokens are banned under every candidate; the remedy is not to gate security-relevant steps on a label, whether a type, a `Changelog:` value or a change file. A consumer-contract concern, not a type penalty.
7. **Release tools need the type in the subject.** Overstated: body notes, `Release-As`, PR-body overrides and change files exist (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/googleapis/release-please/main/README.md; https://changesets.dev/guide/why). release-please, semantic-release's default preset, cocogitto, git-cliff and release-plz do read header types by default; Nx and Lerna only when enabled. Not counted.
8. **Agents propagate it.** Prevalence is measured (65% of Claude-attributed commits); the cause is not, and the shipped harness text does not prescribe it (https://registry.npmjs.org/@anthropic-ai/claude-code/-/claude-code-2.1.49.tgz; https://raw.githubusercontent.com/anomalyco/opencode/aec0b9a6d8898f68f923aaf08b7306d931fd9d76/packages/opencode/src/tool/shell/shell.txt). It cuts both ways: a cost to suppress, and no evidence the prior is accurate.
9. **Parser fragmentation.** Verified: `feat!:` is not matched by the default Angular preset; the spaced `BREAKING CHANGE:` is not a Git trailer (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/conventional-changelog/conventional-changelog/master/packages/conventional-changelog-angular/src/parser.js; https://git-scm.com/docs/git-interpret-trailers). An integration obligation: the typed profile is bound to a named parser and validated by fixture, as this format's own `Breaking-Change:` trailer already is, since the conventionalcommits preset reads it and Angular's ignores it.

**Costs on both sides.** The verdict's costs, unpriced in the first revision (J2): two subject grammars across the adopting repositories wherever the typed profile is adopted, with a different hook regex and PR-title rule; a hook that rejects a prior carried by 65% of one agent's sampled commits, with the rejection-and-retry cost unmeasured; no reader handle for enumerating changes by kind, since `Fixes:` is present only where an introducing commit was substantiated. The type's costs: a non-derivable intent classification on every commit; one type per squash landing under a priority order; per-type truth obligations that fall on review for every commit; `feat` as the residual for every behaviour change that is neither repair nor performance; two breaking markers on every break, and a parser profile for the Angular preset. Neither column is measured. The verdict rests on the mechanism points above, and the inventory threshold that would flip it is named at the end.

**The comparator now drafted.** The equally grounded typed rival is no longer missing from the record. R1 and R2 drafted it: eight Angular types with no residual, a purpose-over-object decision order, a priority rule for tangled changes, type-keyed body and evidence obligations (R2), `!` paired with the breaking trailer, and Angular-preset handling by profile. Its defects as the panel found them: no residual type, so `feat` absorbs removals and requested default changes (J4); split-first on tangled commits with the share unestimated (J4); two breaking markers on every break (J4); `!` unparsed by the Angular preset without reconfiguration, and in R2 a pairing rule its own Angular profile cannot satisfy (J1 and J3, who flagged it fatal as written; J4); a priority order misattributed to Open edX (J1, J2, J4); a circular `refactor` rule (J2, J4); a `perf` obligation that demands figures the evidence rule lets the writer not have (J1, J2); no purpose type defined for contract-bearing documentation (J2); an enumeration command that misses its own unscoped form (J1, J3, J4); and compatibility claims for Nx and Lerna that overlook opt-in activation and the Angular default (J1, J2, J3, J4). The typed header profile above takes what survived and repairs what did not, so the next revision does not re-derive them. What remains the largest gap is the controlled comparison between the two, now specified with three arms below.

## Alternatives and when they would win

**P2, Grounded Commit Messages (writers; second, mean rank 2.25, no fatal flags).** Optional area; the widest evidence trigger; soft budgets; prose enforcement; no universal breaking key. It wins where no area list can be maintained and derivation yields unusable names, or where hook enforcement is impossible and landing review is the only gate. Its evidence grammar and release fixtures are already in the recommendation.

**P7, Compact rationale (readers; third).** Prose verification by default with trailers as an opt-in profile; a computed path map that prefixes the subject only when every changed path maps to one label; versioned profiles. It wins where ceremony must be lowest, no bulk audit of verification claims is wanted, and a human reviews every landing. J3 ranked it first in rounds two and three and called it the most followable specification; J2 flagged its default as fatal for agent-majority repositories because the trust signal and the filter handle both depend on opt-ins.

**P8, area-led subject with evidence trailers (readers; fourth).** P4's design with path derivation and a reader protocol. Its final round requires parent-absent, commit-present substantiation for `Fixes:` and says blame alone only locates a candidate; what cost it the place were enforcement contradictions (a hook rejecting its own profile (c) and native subjects, an incomplete closing-keyword regex, no multi-area rule) and overstated claims about clone completeness and squash unreachability. Its derivation is grafted above.

**R1, Rich Conventional Commits (advocacy; `gpt-6-astra`).** This report's body, trailers, checks and landing rules under a `type(area)!?: outcome` header with Angular's eight types, a mandatory scope, a body on every ordinary commit including trivial ones, and the specification's uppercase `BREAKING-CHANGE:` footer. Its strongest case, stated in its own terms: a reviewed kind can be reused across areas by every later reader without reconstructing intent from prose; an explicit kind may help a writer separate adding, correcting and preserving behaviour before describing the outcome; retaining the syntax most agent output already carries may be cheaper than enforcing its removal; and the measured criticisms of the core specification do not reach an information-matched rival. Every one of those is marked opinion in R1 and it concedes it does not know whether the type's incremental value exceeds its authoring and review cost. No judge found a factual error in it; two scored it the better-grounded of the two drafts and the better basis for a typed profile; its mandatory trivial bodies and two breaking markers are deliberate costs it does not weigh (J1, J3, J4). It wins where a repository has a documented type consumer, which is the typed header profile, whose vocabulary and breaking-marker handling come from it. It would win as the default on a pre-registered net-benefit result from the three-arm trial, or on an inventory showing the typed profile is the majority grammar.

**R2, Rich Conventional Commits (advocacy; `claude-fable-5-1`).** The same header with the parentheses omitted for `all`, a purpose-over-object decision procedure, a priority order for tangled changes, a per-type obligations table, `!` paired with `Breaking-Change:`, and the evidence pair on every `fix`, `perf` and `refactor` whatever the paths. Its contribution is the type-as-claim design and the drift monitor, both grafted. Its unresolved defects, recorded so they are not re-derived: the priority order is its own, not Open edX's; the `refactor` rule forbids the type under uncertainty that the obligations table permits; the `perf` body demands figures its evidence rule allows the writer not to have; contract-bearing documentation has no purpose type; the `!`/trailer pairing conflicts with its own Angular-preset profile; its enumeration command misses its own unscoped form; and its compatibility list overlooks Lerna's Angular default and Nx's opt-in. Its writer case rests on transferring annotator agreement measured on single-type commits by the definition authors to agents writing tangled commits, which its own concessions undercut (J2, J4). J2 called it the better-engineered rival; J1 and J3 flagged its Angular route fatal as written and scored it well below R1.

**Plain Git, kernel, Go or Google prose, and Scoped Commits** (https://git-scm.com/docs/SubmittingPatches; https://docs.kernel.org/process/submitting-patches.html; https://google.github.io/eng-practices/review/developer/cl-descriptions.html; https://scopedcommits.com/). The recommendation descends from these and adds requirements they leave optional (a body on most changes, an evidence account), so it is not compatible with their rules. Prose can disclose a missing check, as the kernel policy requires and P7 shows; what these formats lack is a standard key, compulsory presence and a hook-checkable grammar. They win where humans write most commits and review catches omissions, a proposed use condition rather than a demonstrated threshold (J1).

**Changesets, towncrier, version plans; GitLab `Changelog:` trailer.** Release profiles (a) and (b), complementary. Change files win for multi-package release units where a reviewed package-to-bump file is the only deterministic contract (https://changesets.dev/guide/why; https://towncrier.readthedocs.io/en/stable/); the trailer wins where GitLab's generator or git-cliff footer parsing is the consumer (https://docs.gitlab.com/user/project/changelogs/).

**Lore** (https://arxiv.org/html/2603.15566v1). Its `Tested`/`Not-tested` idea is the ancestor of the evidence pair; rejected for its `Directive` channel and an evaluation proposed but not run.

**Qualified Agent Commits, Agentic Commits, Gitmoji, issue-key prefixes, Git notes.** QAC reads absent trailers as human authorship, unsound outside full compliance (https://github.com/hubtheocoelho/qac-spec/blob/master/SPECIFICATION.md); Agentic Commits publishes accuracy percentages with no method (https://deligoz.me/projects/agentic-commits/); Gitmoji is a type vocabulary with rendering variance (https://gitmoji.dev/specification); issue-key prefixes bind the subject to one tracker; Git notes are not fetched by an ordinary clone (https://git-scm.com/docs/git-notes). None wins here.

## Dissent

The panel never reached three first-place votes. In round three J2 and J4 ranked P4 first; J1 ranked P2 first; J3 ranked P7 first, then P2. The margin between P4 (mean rank 2.00) and P2 (2.25) is one place on one ballot. J3 flagged P4 as fatal for demanding that every profile's breaking-change fixture "yield a major", impossible for a changelog-only consumer; the recommendation repairs this with P2's and P7's separation of version selection from changelog inclusion. P2 carried no fatal flag. J2 and J4 called P4 the most followable specification and P2 the best-grounded; J1 and J3 scored P4 below P2 and P7 on precision (J1: 6 against 8 and 8; J3: 5 against 8 and 9), and J3 called P7 the most followable. The recommendation takes P4's shape with P2's grammar grafted in; that the repaired text is the more followable is the synthesiser's judgement, since no judge assessed it.

A fact of the record: all twelve first-place votes across three rounds went to a proposal from the judge's own model family, and two of the three disagreements left open in round three fall on that line. This report resolves them on the evidence stated, not on the vote count; the alignment is reported so the reader can weigh it.

**The type verdict.** Both advocacy challengers, and J1 and J3 across all three rounds of the main exercise, found the Conventional Commits verdict argued asymmetrically: a type was required to demonstrate a benefit while the area, the trailer block and the hard cap were accepted on mechanism. The synthesiser accepted the point and restated the basis as a cost judgement without changing the default; J2 and J4 reached the same conclusion from the other family. The advocacy panel's family alignment is also a fact of the record: the two GPT judges scored the GPT advocate's draft well above the Claude advocate's and flagged the latter's Angular route fatal; the two Claude judges split, J2 preferring the Claude draft and J4 giving the Claude draft the higher agent-reader score and the GPT draft the higher grounding and precision scores while calling the Claude draft the stronger argument (repair: CHECK-3 F8). No judge of either family voted to overturn. The same uncertainty standard now applies to this format's own mandatory area, evidence ceremony and hard cap, each of which is provisional on the terms stated in the answer (J1, J3).

**Mandatory area or optional.** P4 and P8 required one; P2 permitted an established name; P7 computed one only when a map resolves every path. J2 and J4 favoured a token always present, because retrieval selects lexically first and one rule keeps writers consistent; J1 and J3 favoured optional areas because mandatory selection adds an unvalidated classification obligation. Resolved: mandatory, with derivation and `all` as fallback, on mechanism and practice with no measurement either way; the focal choice on a multi-area change remains a judgement, conceding part of J1's and J3's point. Two against two; genuinely contested.

**Verification as trailers or prose.** P2, P4 and P8 used trailers; P7 prose with an opt-in profile. J2 and J4 favoured trailers because presence checks and extraction exist for trailers alone and P2's grammar makes filler visible; J1 narrowly preferred P2's common extraction contract as one contract shared across repositories, conditional on monitoring cost; J3 preferred P7, calling trailers justified only where a bulk-audit consumer exists. J1 and J3 rejected P4's claim that prose gives an agent "nothing to abstain into"; this report drops it. Resolved: trailers by default, on mechanism and hook-checkability; three to one, with J1's qualification, and no filler or fabrication measurement under either (repair: CHECK-2 F4).

**Hard 72-character subject or soft budget.** J2 and J4 favoured the hard cap as the cheapest check against documented displays; J1 and J3 held that those displays establish no universal threshold and that an indispensable identifier should be allowed to overrun. Resolved: hard 72, aim 60; the cost of an occasional truncated identifier is unmeasured. Two against two.

**Breaking-change trailer always, or the consumer's marker only.** P4 and P8 always wrote `Breaking-Change:`; P2 called a universal key "inert"; P7 put the break first in the body. J4 showed from parser source that the conventionalcommits preset reads the key; J1 and J3 accepted this but saw an unmeasured duplication cost. Resolved: keep the trailer, add the parser's marker under the typed profile. Weakly contested.

**`Fixes:` or `Introduced-by:`.** J4 recommended renaming because the word closes issues on GitHub and in Go and means an incomplete fix in dpkg (https://wiki.debian.org/Teams/Dpkg/GitUsage; https://go.dev/wiki/CommitMessage). Resolved: keep `Fixes:` on kernel precedent, with the issue-number form hook-rejected on every forge's effective pattern; the rename stays open and the advocacy panel did not touch it.

**Dependency bots.** P4 exempts listed bot authors at commit time; P8 configures bot prefixes and has the landing agent add the evidence pair; P2 and P7 require repair at landing with no exemption, which J1 and J3 preferred because producer identity does not establish unchanged behaviour. Resolved: P4's exemption, never from the token rule, with repair at landing; the workload is unmeasured.

What remains genuinely contested is whether the recurring ceremony (an area on every subject, an evidence pair on every code-touching commit, claim-keyed obligations, a hook, a profile, a landing read-back) repays itself where agents write most of the history. Every judge in both panels said the benefit is unmeasured; two argued from constraint of the writer, two from unproven cost, and the advocacy judges added that the same is true of the type on the other side.

## What would change the answer

- **A controlled comparison with three arms**, isolating the type from the scope CoCoRerank bundles with it: this format; the typed header on the same body, evidence rules and triviality policy; and the typed header with per-type obligations. Reader and writer experiments run separately. The reader trial holds outcome text, bodies, evidence, code and retrieval tooling constant and varies only the header, measuring agent bisection, localisation and intent reconstruction, human triage time, and recognition of neutral absolute references by a link extractor such as RepoMem's. The writer trial randomises matched tasks with identical briefs, observations and tools between instructions, blind-scoring narration, unsupported motive, missing identifiers, `Verified:` filler and classification disagreements, with realistic wrong labels, mixed changes, unknown context, dependency updates, native reverts, squash and backports included. Workload weights and unacceptable regressions, an increase in incorrect release decisions or in unsupported-motive rate among them, are pre-registered; the default flips to the typed profile on a net-benefit result under those weights, not on any single improved metric (repair: J1, J2, J3, J4; R1's design). Both formats' authoring, retrieval, repair and configuration costs are measured in the same trial, with token counts under a named tokeniser.
- **A live type-disagreement rate** on real agent commits written under the typed profile in the adopting repositories, scored against adjudicated labels, with the decision threshold stated before the sample is drawn. R2's proposed 10% is not adopted: no loss function connects it to release harm or repair effort (J1). Beside it, the live area-disagreement rate under this format, so the two classifications are held to one standard.
- **A fix-enumeration trial.** On a squash-landed agent history with adjudicated defect repairs, the precision and recall of `^fix\(` against keyword search over this format's subjects and against reading bodies. If the token does not beat keyword search materially, the kind-enumeration trigger for the typed profile loses its reader case; if it does, the cost the default accepts is quantified.
- **A consumer inventory**, with the decision rule set before it is read: which repositories bind a type-reading tool, and which record a non-release type consumer (an index, a report, a review workflow); which squash, through the web form or the API; what share of commits come from writers the guidance never reaches. The proposed rule, opinion: if more than half of the commit volume in scope lands in repositories that take the typed profile, the typed header becomes the default and the area-only header the profile, because the second grammar is then the majority grammar. The inventory also prices the two grammar profiles against a universal taxonomy, the two costs both panels said were unpriced.
- **A scored sample of real agent commits from the adopting repositories** for diff narration, unsupported motive, `Verified:` specificity and filler, and claim-obligation compliance. If the nullable pair attracts filler faster than it buys disclosure, or offers an easy exit from genuine checks, the evidence trigger narrows towards P7's. Named as the decisive local test by P4, P7, the gap analysis and three judges.
- **A tokeniser count over a real history** in each candidate shape, under a named tokeniser; if bodies dominate retrieval cost with no benefit, the budget tightens.
- **Harness-captured verification.** Record each command and exit status and compare with `Verified:`; if self-reports diverge materially, the trailer becomes a pointer to captured evidence.
- **Drift measurement.** If list-bound or derived areas drift faster than optional ones, or `all` dominates, the token becomes optional; under the typed profile, if the `feat` share rises or `refactor` vanishes, the type is sampled against bodies and the priority order revisited.

## Method

The exercise ran in six stages on 2 October 2026. Proposals, judges, advocacy drafts and cross-checks are cited above by label (P1 to P8, J1 to J4, R1 and R2, CHECK-1 to CHECK-4, a check's findings numbered F1 onwards). Those intermediate documents, the evidence notes and the run records are not reproduced here, so every claim above cites its primary source directly.

**Collection.** A prior single-worker survey was treated as one voice to check; five of its claims were found wrong. Seven collection angles ran independently (Conventional Commits, alternatives, writers, readers, machine consumers, measurement literature, workflow first principles); a completeness critic named six gaps, each then filled; a claim verifier spot-checked twenty primary and thirteen secondary claims, and every measurement figure held. Every source carries an access date and an evidence tag; local measurements were read-only parser fixtures on Git 2.55.0, and no forge, release tool or agent was exercised.

**Proposals.** Eight proposals were written blind from four vantage points, each by two model families: sceptic (P1, P5), writers (P2, P4), machines (P3, P6), readers (P7, P8). By authorship: P1, P4, P6 and P8 by `claude-fable-5-1`; P2, P3, P5 and P7 by `gpt-6-astra`. Round one eliminated P1, P3, P5 and P6. Rounds two and three revised the four finalists, who could borrow from one another with attribution; by round three they had converged on one design, which every judge discounted as lineage rather than confirmation.

**Judging.** Four judges assessed each round blind to authorship, scoring seven dimensions, listing fatal flaws, factual errors and graft-worthy ideas, and ranking. J1 and J3 ran on `gpt-6-astra`; J2 and J4 on `claude-fable-5-1`. Convergence required three first-place votes and was never reached. Final standing: P4 (mean rank 2.00, cross-family 3.00, two firsts, one fatal flag), P2 (2.25, 3.00, one, none), P7 (2.75, 4.00, one, one), P8 (3.00, 4.00, none, two). Two judges reported the authorship mapping reachable from their working directory and said they did not use it. Every first-place vote in every round went to the judge's own family.

**Synthesis and first check.** The specification takes P4 as its base, applies the grafts at least two judges endorsed, and repairs every defect a judge established against P4, each marked in the text. An independent cross-check of the first draft (CHECK-1) returned seven major and three minor findings; all ten were verified against the sources and accepted.

**Second check.** A second independent cross-check of the revised report (CHECK-2) returned three major findings, each a contract defect in the recommended format (the issue-closing ban was GitHub's list presented as a universal rule; area normalisation could yield a subject the hook rejects; a copied `final contents` claim had two meanings after a rewrite), and two minor (the headline misstated the trailers-versus-prose split; the RepoMem link-extraction caveat was dropped). All five were checked against the cited sources and accepted; the format, not only the prose, was changed. The synthesiser re-fetched GitLab's and Forgejo's reference documentation to confirm the keyword lists written into the contract, the only new fetches in this revision; every other source added here was fetched by the checker or by an advocacy judge on the same day.

**Advocacy round.** Because every original proposal rejected the typed header, two advocates, R1 on `gpt-6-astra` and R2 on `claude-fable-5-1`, independently drafted Rich Conventional Commits on this report's baseline and argued where its verdict fails. Four judges, J1 and J3 on `gpt-6-astra` and J2 and J4 on `claude-fable-5-1`, read the brief, the report and both drafts blind to authorship, scored the three candidates on the same seven dimensions, listed fatal flaws and factual errors, fetched the decisive primary sources themselves (Zeng et al., CoCoRerank, Robbes et al., OEP-51, the Conventional Commits specification, the parser and analyser sources, Lerna's and Nx's documentation), and returned a verdict of overturn, qualify or hold with the changes each required. Tally: four qualify, no overturn, no hold. No judge found a fatal flaw in the report's format or in R1; J1 and J3 flagged R2's Angular route fatal as written. The judges' required changes were applied in this revision as marked (graft: R1, R2; repair: J1 to J4), and the reasoning the panel found asymmetric was replaced.

**Third check.** A third independent cross-check of the report and the guidance distillation (CHECK-3) returned four major findings (a valid derived area could exceed the subject cap; the claim-keyed obligations omitted the `result unavailable` state; the distillation had dropped the partial-revert safeguard and the full-message read-back) and four minor (a wrong reorder example beside the tree-equality rule; the CoCoRerank case study clipped to its adverse half; squash landing stated as a population property; J4's advocacy scores misdescribed). All eight were checked against the sources and accepted, none going to a disagreement note: the area grammar gained a 24-character bound with the subject feasibility stated per header, every obligation accepts either declared gap form, and the distillation regained both safeguards. The synthesiser re-fetched the CoCoRerank case study, the git-rebase manual and Robbes et al.'s extraction method to confirm the wording written in.

**This revision's checks.** The five worked messages were passed to `git interpret-trailers --parse` on Git 2.55.0 on standard input with every intended key extracted, every `Verified:` value yielding three parts under the first-and-last-semicolon split, and every line within 72 columns; the rewrite fragment in the evidence rules is the trailer block of the fifth message and was checked there; the normalisation, subject-length, unavailable-result and actionable-reference fixtures were evaluated as in-memory string checks against the documented grammars. No repository, hook, forge action, release tool, rewrite or agent was exercised, and none of this establishes the fictional checks' truth. The one-screen distillation for agent guidance that J4 asked for is `GUIDANCE-DRAFT.md`, written from this revision.

A fourth cross-check (CHECK-4) of this revision returned one major finding, the `build.gradle` root-stem case against the `ci`/`docs`/`test`/`build` eligibility rule, and three minor ones (the distillation's fixed rewrite fallback, the Codoban testimony claim, the enumeration argument's universal precondition). The four repairs were applied by hand, marked `repair: CHECK-4` in the text, and no fifth check was run.
